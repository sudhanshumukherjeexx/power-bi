After the Finance dispute (Sprint 02) the sales dashboard shows both Net Sales and the ledger's Net Revenue. Then the leadership meeting got three different Q1 revenue figures from three different reports.

Why the business cares When three reports disagree, every meeting starts with an argument about numbers instead of decisions. Each copy of the model also costs refresh time, capacity and maintenance, and each one can drift further.

KMKenji MoriData Engineerchat Mon 11 May, 11:12

FYI the Ops model reads from the shipments table in the warehouse, not orders. Ops counts revenue when stuff leaves the building. Their dashboard drives the warehouse staffing plan, so don't break it.

What you are expected to do

  • Inventory the three models and their reports: what each Revenue measure means, who owns it, who depends on it.
  • Explain exactly why the three values differ (definitions, not bugs).
  • Design one shared, certified semantic model: its tables, its measures (with unambiguous names) and which one is the company's "Revenue".
  • Plan the migration of every report to thin reports on the shared model, in an order that never breaks a business process.
  • Define ownership: who owns each definition, who maintains the model, who can certify and who approves changes.

Deliverables

  • Inventory of models, measures, reports and owners
  • KPI dictionary with each revenue definition, its owner and its use
  • Shared model design: tables, grain, measures, security
  • Migration plan: report by report, with rollback
  • Ownership matrix (RACI) for definitions and the model
  • Short decision record: one shared model vs departmental models
